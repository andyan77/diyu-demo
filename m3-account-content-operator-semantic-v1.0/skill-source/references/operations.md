# Operating Decisions

Load this reference for account-state diagnosis, cycle planning, daily decisions, review updates, Campaign overlays, or Content Brief handoff.

## 1. Decision order

Use this order; skip irrelevant steps:

1. Identify the authorized objective, priorities, non-sacrifice conditions, platform, and current scope.
2. Interpret maturity separately from temporary objective overlays and health hypotheses.
3. Check actual capacity, usable facts/assets, permissions, and fulfillment paths.
4. Decide the minimum sufficient cycle mix and resource order.
5. Balance reliable delivery with bounded exploration.
6. Check continuity obligations and Campaign coverage.
7. Produce tasks only where production is supported.
8. On review, update the smallest affected judgment or keep it unchanged with reasons.

## 2. Goal semantics

| Objective | Required operating distinction | Common false substitute |
|---|---|---|
| Long-term value | durable trust, judgment value, and reasons to return | vague “quality content” with no durable promise |
| Cold-start | reduce account viability uncertainty and validate follow reasons | maximizing raw views without learning |
| Follower growth | create an ongoing follow reason and future expectation | treating any engagement as useful followers |
| Traffic | expand qualified reach and distribution opportunity | generic controversy or broad reach without account fit |
| GMV | reduce buying friction through a valid sales path | high-intent copy with unavailable stock or no fulfillment |
| Leads | create identifiable, serviceable inquiry intent | comments/likes counted as leads |
| Store visit | make a specific physical visit valuable and feasible | copying e-commerce conversion logic without location/time/store reality |
| Mixed | assign non-interchangeable roles and explicit priorities | every task serving every objective |

When two objectives conflict, state the trade-off and recommendation. Ask the user only if the unresolved choice changes product meaning or a protected priority; ordinary allocation remains a professional operating decision.

## 3. Stage interpretation

Do not infer stage from a single metric. Consider:

- account history and consistency;
- whether a credible follow reason exists;
- repeatability of content mechanisms;
- audience response quality, not only volume;
- fulfillment and production stability;
- objective and observation window;
- recent disruptions and alternative explanations.

Interpret:

- **Cold-start**: core account-content assumptions remain largely untested.
- **Direction validation**: some hypotheses have observations, but repeatability or audience fit remains uncertain.
- **Growth**: usable mechanisms exist and the primary problem is qualified expansion without destroying identity.
- **Stable operation**: identity, production, audience expectation, and operating mechanisms are sufficiently repeatable for ongoing management.
- **Conversion sprint**: a temporary priority overlay; it can occur at multiple maturity levels if fulfillment is real.
- **Adjustment**: a planned response, not a maturity state.
- **Decline**: a provisional health interpretation requiring windowed evidence and confound checks.

If evidence supports multiple readings, give the competing interpretations and identify one discriminating observation. Do not force a single label.

## 4. Capacity and cadence

Maintain three values conceptually:

- expected output requested by the user;
- baseline sustainable capacity;
- actual capacity in the current window.

When expected output exceeds actual capacity:

1. preserve the primary objective and highest-value task;
2. remove interchangeable or low-information tasks;
3. simplify the realization approach without changing the business job;
4. defer or replace tasks whose conditions are absent;
5. explain the lost benefit or delayed learning;
6. request more resource only when it changes the feasible frontier.

Do not evenly degrade every task. Do not translate “three per day” into “three per week.” Use the user’s actual time unit.

## 5. Stable delivery and exploration

For each repeatable operating or expression mechanism, reason on two separate dimensions:

### Evidence maturity

- no observation;
- limited observation;
- repeated observation;
- contradicted or stale evidence.

### Current use decision

- explore;
- maintain;
- expand;
- pause;
- redesign;
- exit.

These are explanatory semantics, not required database enums. Choose only distinctions that change a decision.

For every exploration proposal record:

- the uncertainty it addresses;
- the smallest reversible action;
- the observation that would support continuation;
- the observation that would refute or redesign it;
- its expiry or review condition;
- what stable obligation it displaces, if any.

Exploration need not always be nonzero. Stable delivery need not always be nonzero. Never freeze a universal ratio.

## 6. Content mix

Create the smallest set of distinct business jobs needed in the current window. Possible roles include stable delivery, exploration, long-term accumulation, conversion support, lead capture, or store activation. These are not mandatory slots.

For every proposed task ask:

- What unique business job does it perform?
- What changes if it is removed?
- Is another task interchangeable with it?
- Is production supported now?
- Which observation can change the next decision?

Delete or merge tasks that make no difference under the frozen objective.

## 7. Continuity obligation

Recompute continuity from evidence; do not persist a permanent “audience promise state.” Evidence can include:

- an unclosed public forward promise;
- current column-specific requests or reliance;
- current business paths that depend on the column.

Insufficient alone:

- historic frequency;
- historic performance;
- operator attachment;
- a stale comment without current relevance.

Actions:

- **Shrink**: reduce frequency or scope while keeping the value path intact.
- **Pause**: stop temporarily and preserve recoverability; communicate only when an external expectation exists.
- **Exit**: close the valid promise or migrate the business path; use the highest evidence threshold.

Do not invent a restart date. Return a long-term identity conflict to Matrix.

## 8. Campaign overlay

Represent a Campaign overlay through its bounded business meaning:

- objective and validity window;
- named occupied content positions or resources;
- conflicts with the cycle baseline;
- observation target and exit condition;
- baseline elements that remain active.

During the overlay, M3 manages uncovered positions. At expiry, restore only the still-current baseline. If the baseline changed legitimately during the Campaign, do not resurrect the stale version.

## 9. CTA decision

| CTA class | Condition |
|---|---|
| Follow, comment, save | may be proposed when natural and factually safe; no separate CTA form required |
| Product click, inquiry, lead, store visit, purchase | matching objective plus a current effective fulfillment path |
| External diversion, price offer, strong purchase promise, high-risk expression | explicit authorization and verified conditions |

Never infer risky permission from a traffic, follower, or GMV objective. Do not invent stock, price, promotion, availability, response capacity, or service promises.

## 10. Feedback discipline

Use evidence identities separately:

| Evidence | What it can support | What it cannot support alone |
|---|---|---|
| Real platform observation on an exact publication | observed performance in that window | causal attribution or universal effectiveness |
| Manual platform note | a reported observation | platform-verified metric status |
| Manually entered business outcome | reported inquiry/store/sale outcome | causal attribution to content without stronger design |
| User quality judgment | preference or perceived quality | platform performance |
| External market observation | scoped market hypothesis | account-specific lift |
| Simulated feedback | engineering handling and branch behavior | real market validity |

Update procedure:

1. Identify version, platform, account, window, source, and distribution conditions.
2. State the observation.
3. State plausible interpretations and confounders.
4. Identify the smallest affected decision.
5. Change it, continue observing, or retain it.
6. Preserve unaffected decisions.

## 11. Content task handoff

Emit a task only when it gives Content Brief enough business substance to start. Include:

- task source and cycle role or not-applicable;
- account/expression subject;
- target platform and content form;
- one primary objective and limited secondary contribution;
- priority and non-sacrifice conditions;
- audience problem, scene, or opportunity;
- desired audience change;
- content promise, core proposition, or testable hypothesis;
- usable facts, materials, sources, and gaps;
- relevant product and fulfillment conditions;
- capacity, timing, permission, CTA, risk, and non-promise boundaries;
- observation target and evidence identity.

Do not include hooks, narratives, dialogue, shots, performance direction, title, cover, or final publishing copy.

When production is not supported, emit a no-task decision with the reason and next evidence/action instead of an empty task shell.
