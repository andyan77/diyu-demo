# Fashion and Market Decisions

Load this reference only when apparel products, inventory, try-on, seasons, stores, or external market evidence can change the operating decision. Treat it as a decision aid, not a static platform truth source.

## 1. Build the product-operating picture

Use only relevant, current, sourced facts:

- product stage: launch, active sale, replenishment, promotion, exit, or unknown;
- product relationship: hero item, supporting item, styling complement, store-only item, or another evidenced role;
- regional season and climate rather than a generic national season;
- color × size × channel × time sellability;
- replenishment possibility and exit pressure;
- material, fit, craft, care, and price facts;
- try-on facts and separately available try-on media;
- use scenes and customer decision problems;
- mini-program, inquiry, store, or other fulfillment paths;
- store location, hours, current stock, service capacity, and redeemable experience.

Do not let an unverified industry generalization override product facts.

## 2. Define the content unit

Prefer:

```text
customer decision problem
+ relevant product relationship
+ currently fulfillable path
```

Do not equate a content unit with one SKU or one generic product introduction. A single task may compare products when the customer decision requires comparison. A product may support different tasks when the decision problem or path is materially different.

## 3. Apply local invalidation

| Change or absence | Invalidate or constrain | Preserve |
|---|---|---|
| one color/size becomes unavailable | related sellability, CTA, timing, and affected task | material/craft facts and unrelated variants |
| replenishment becomes uncertain | promise, urgency, and campaign window | verified product facts |
| price or promotion expires | price/offer claim and conversion task | non-price content whose promise remains valid |
| no try-on has occurred | claims requiring on-body evidence | material, detail, care, styling principle, flat-lay, or other supported content |
| try-on occurred but media is unusable | production path requiring that media | verified observations, if separately sourced and permitted |
| store cannot fulfill current demand | store-visit CTA and store-availability claim | non-store paths that remain valid |
| season/climate window changes | scene relevance and timing | evergreen facts not dependent on the window |

Never turn “not currently sellable” into “product facts are false.” Never turn “no try-on media” into “no apparel content can be produced.”

## 4. Use claim-specific evidence

Require evidence that matches the claim:

- body fit, drape, movement, comfort, and proportion need appropriate on-body evidence or explicit uncertainty;
- composition, construction, dimensions, care, price, and stock need current authoritative product data;
- “works for a scene” needs a defensible link between product properties and the scene;
- customer preference needs observation, not a generic fashion rule;
- scarcity, best-seller, flattering, premium, and similar claims need their own support.

When evidence is absent, reduce claim strength, change the task, condition the statement, defer, or do not publish. Do not fill gaps with conservative-sounding numbers or familiar industry tropes.

## 5. Distinguish GMV, leads, and store visits

### GMV

Focus on purchase-decision friction, current sellability, product proof, comparison, price/offer truth, and an actual purchase route.

### Leads

Focus on a useful reason to inquire, the information or service available after inquiry, qualification boundaries, response capacity, and reliable lead capture. A comment or like is not automatically a lead.

### Store visits

Focus on why the physical visit creates value unavailable or weaker online, who should visit, geography and timing, store stock/experience, service capacity, and an honest visit path. Do not infer store readiness from online sellability.

These objectives can coexist, but their tasks and observation targets are not interchangeable.

## 6. Discover opportunity actively

Build an opportunity hypothesis from:

- a current demand, attention, behavior, supply, or timing signal;
- its platform, region, category, audience, and time scope;
- evidence strength and known bias;
- account and expression fit;
- product/operational ability to fulfill;
- a distinct customer decision problem or value gap;
- the smallest reversible content/operating action;
- expected observable response and refutation;
- expiry or review condition.

Possible decisions:

- screen out;
- observe;
- run a bounded validation;
- exploit within current capacity;
- stop or redesign.

Do not confuse:

- **opportunity**: scoped signal + fit + supply/path + reversible test;
- **trend chasing**: attention signal without account or business fit;
- **homogeneity defense**: avoiding crowded expression without evidence of positive demand;
- **unsuitable opportunity**: market signal the account cannot credibly express or fulfill.

## 7. Layer homogeneity responsibility

| Layer | Owner | M3 action |
|---|---|---|
| category/track opportunity, topic saturation, content-pillar balance, column fatigue, cycle concentration | M3 | alter the cycle mix, timing, resource level, or decision to validate |
| single-content angle, hook, narrative, and expression mechanism | Creative Tournament / Creative Script | preserve room for real alternatives; do not prescribe the mechanism |
| title, cover, entry promise, and platform packaging | Publishing & Packaging | pass facts and constraints; do not write the package |

Reliable, current, scoped external evidence may justify lightweight reversible action without evidence of account decline. Permanent exit or long-term repositioning requires stronger account-specific evidence; repositioning returns to Matrix.

If market evidence is absent, production may continue from internal evidence, but state that external comparison is incomplete. Never claim platform uniqueness or scarcity without current scoped evidence.

## 8. Bound tools and freshness

Use current platform and market sources at runtime when the decision depends on them. Do not copy volatile platform rules into this reference. Preserve source, platform, observation time, scope, and freshness.

Do not build an automatic monitoring, scoring, or optimization platform for the first slice. Add a minimal deterministic helper only when real operation demonstrates a need for date windows, capacity arithmetic, or deduplication; prefer the data/application layer over the professional Skill.
